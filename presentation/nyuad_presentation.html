RESEARCH TOPICS

Main research topics I work with:

GUIDANCE VECTOR FIELDS (GVF)


  • Essentially simple algorithms for curve tracking, yet endowed with mathematical guarantees.

  • My first published work on this topic (IEEE Transactions on Robotics) dates back to 2010, when I was an undergraduate student. Since then, many extensions have followed. The most recent work (currently under review) extends the framework to curves on Lie groups:
    BARTELT, F.; GONÇALVES, V. M.; PIMENTA, L. C. A. Constructive Path-Tracking and Circulating Vector Fields in Matrix Lie Groups. Automatica (under review).

  • The approach is highly practical — many projects in my lab, including collaborations with private companies, use our GVF algorithms. In fact, there is a pending patent in partnership with VALE, a major Brazilian mining company.

  • I will present this topic in an upcoming IROS 2025 Workshop.

PETRI NET/TROPICAL ALGEBRA CONTROL


  • This is my most mathematical research area: controlling Petri nets using the framework of Tropical Algebra.

  • It has applications in synchronization problems — the classical example being the synchronization of trains in a network to minimize passenger waiting times. However, its practical use is still quite limited due to the stringent conditions required for its application.

  • I am also interested in Tropical Algebra itself, independently of its control applications. Early in my career, I published papers in mathematics journals on algorithms for solving algebraic problems such as tropical linear systems.

  • Although I now work on this topic less frequently, my most recent paper — in collaboration with NYU Abu Dhabi — is quite recent:
    GONÇALVES, V. M.; KRISHNAMURTHY, P.; TZES, A.; KHORRAMI, F. Control of Max-Plus Linear Systems Using Feedback Cycle Shaping. Automatica (2025).


CONTROL BARRIER FUNCTIONS (CBFs)

  • A very popular and practical technique for incorporating inequality constraints into a control loop.

  • The simplest example is obstacle avoidance, where the goal is to maintain the distance \(D\) greater than a safety threshold, \(D > D_{safe}\).

  • I had already worked on this topic before joining NYU Abu Dhabi, but my involvement intensified significantly after starting there. Through this collaboration, we have published five journal papers on the subject, with others currently under review.

  • Two key highlights from this collaboration: first, we proposed a simple modification to the traditional CBF framework to improve its success rate; second, this line of work directly inspired my next research topic...

DIFFERENTIABLE DISTANCES

  • While working with CBFs at NYU Abu Dhabi on obstacle avoidance, I noticed that in some cases the controller exhibited poor performance (very noisy control inputs) or required extremely high control efforts.

  • I realized this occurred because the algorithm relied on derivatives of the Euclidean distance — but the Euclidean distance is not always differentiable!

  • This observation led to my research on developing an alternative to the Euclidean distance that is smooth (e.g., differentiable infinitely or at least a finite number of times).

  • This is a fascinating topic, both from practical and theoretical perspectives, and it appears to be largely underexplored.

DIFFERENTIABLE DISTANCES

  • We have already developed promising algorithms for convex-to-convex distance computation, currently under review.

  • We are also working on convex-to-point cloud distance computation, which is highly useful in practical applications, but there remains significant room for improvement in both computational efficiency and theoretical understanding.

  • In my research proposal, I outline several goals related to differentiable distance metrics, including key research questions and potential applications.
